💡 Part A — Digital Inputs vs Outputs

Remember

A digital output pin sends electricity OUT (e.g. LED, buzzer). A digital input pin READS electricity coming in (e.g. button). Both use the Pin class from machine but with different modes.

🔌 Part B — Wiring Checklist

Complete each step in order. DO NOT power on until you have ticked every step in Stage 1.

Stage 1 — Pushbutton

  • Pushbutton placed in the breadboard across the centre gap
  • Jumper wire from Button leg 1 → GP14 on the Pico W
  • Jumper wire from Button leg 2 → 3.3V pin on the Pico W
  • 10kΩ resistor from Button leg 1 row → GND rail on the breadboard
  • Checked with teacher — wiring is correct
  • Tested in Shell: from machine import Pin; b = Pin(14, Pin.IN, Pin.PULL_DOWN); print(b.value()) → prints 0 when not pressed, 1 when held

Stage 2 — Active Buzzer

  • Buzzer placed in breadboard with + (longer leg) identified
  • + leg connected to GP15 on the Pico W
  • – leg connected to GND rail
  • Tested in Shell: from machine import Pin; bz = Pin(15, Pin.OUT); bz.value(1) → buzzer makes sound
  • bz.value(0) turns it off
  • Photographed the breadboard for folio
